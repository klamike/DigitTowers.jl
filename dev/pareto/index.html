When one cost is not enough

Merging digits and adding plus signs are different ways to repair a tower. The cheapest repair depends on how much each operation costs. A Pareto frontier keeps every tradeoff: an expression survives if no other expression uses no more of both operations and strictly fewer of at least one.

using DigitTowers

r = synthesize_frontier(4294967296; roots=[9], max_base_width=1)
[(c.concatenations, c.additions) for c in r.certificates]
# [(2, 6), (3, 5)]
all(verify, r.certificates)
# true

Here 4294967296 = 2^32. Both ninth-root expressions have outer base 4 and exponent 144. They offer a real choice:

\[4294967296 =\sqrt[9]{4^{2+9+49+67+2+9+6}} =\sqrt[9]{4^{29+4+9+67+29+6}}.\]

The first expression joins two boundaries and adds six plus signs; the second joins three boundaries and adds five plus signs. Exponentiation and addition still follow the same right-associated grammar as synthesize; sums are displayed without redundant parentheses because addition is associative.

A repair that every weighted sum misses

Varying the scalar weights in synthesize does not necessarily reveal the whole frontier. Consider 2199023255552 = 2^41, with root six, outer base two, blocks of at most three digits, and ^/+ in its exponent. Its exact frontier is:

Joined boundariesPlus signsExponent expression
28199+0+2+3+2+5+5+5+5^2
471+9+90+2+32+5+55+52
5619+90+2+3+25+55+52

Each exponent equals 246, so every expression evaluates to root_6(2^246) = 2^41. The middle repair is nondominated, but it cannot minimize any nonnegative weighted sum with at least one positive weight. Let w be the cost of a join divided by the cost of a plus sign. To beat the first repair, the middle one needs w ≤ 1/2; to beat the last, it needs w ≥ 1. These conditions conflict. Setting either weight to zero selects an endpoint.

r = synthesize_frontier(2199023255552; roots=[6], max_base_width=1)
[(c.concatenations, c.additions) for c in r.certificates]
# [(2, 8), (4, 7), (5, 6)]

Such an unsupported tradeoff matters when the constraints are budgets rather than prices. With at most four joins and seven plus signs, only the middle repair is feasible. A scalar optimizer can miss that choice however its weights are tuned.

An exact chart of antichains

The arithmetic step is unchanged: write the outer base as b=a^g and require the represented number to be N=a^h. For root r, the exponent must equal E=r*h/g, an integer. The semantic suffix chart caps values at maximum(E)+1, sharing its work across requested root orders.

For each suffix position and capped value, the chart stores an antichain of (joins, plus signs) costs, with one deterministic witness per pair. Combining a block with a suffix adds nonnegative costs coordinate by coordinate. A dominated suffix can therefore never improve a future prefix: replacing it with its dominator preserves the exact value and weakly improves both costs. This proves that discarding dominated labels preserves every final Pareto cost. Saturation still preserves zero, one, and undefined 0^0 exactly.

The result contains a flat certificates vector and frontiers grouped by root and outer-base width. These groups optimize separately. Two different roots or outer-base widths are not compared against each other. Every certificate uses the existing verify and expression interfaces; its cost field is simply concatenations + additions.

states counts retained Pareto labels across all suffix charts; value_states counts semantic (position,value) cells. max_states bounds the labels, which may be more numerous than scalar chart states. An explicit limit violation throws an error. A returned complete=true applies to the specified finite grammar and all requested roots. Equal-cost syntactic variants are represented by one witness, rather than enumerated.

Census

For 2–30 decimal digits, external roots 2–9, blocks of at most three digits, no leading zeros in blocks, and forbidden 0^0, the complete census gives:

QuantityCount
Arithmetically possible numbers67
Representable numbers52
Feasible root/base-width pairs191
Pairs with multiple nondominated costs112
Nondominated certificates442
Unsupported Pareto points19
Semantic chart states57,063
Pareto labels124,838

The represented numbers match the scalar synthesis census; the extra output describes choices that a single cost hides. Unsupported points were checked with exact rational inequalities, including ties, rather than a finite grid of trial weights.

julia --project=. research/pareto_experiments.jl

research/results/pareto_census.tsv records every witness and whether a weighted objective can support its cost. pareto_metadata.toml gives the full search domain. Tests independently enumerate digit partitions and operator strings, evaluate their values, and discard dominated costs only after that exhaustive enumeration.
