Towers form a language

A surprisingly small piece of automata theory explains when a tower is even defined. For a right-associated tower of nonnegative integer tokens, with 0^0 forbidden:

\[\text{the tower is defined}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{no two adjacent tokens are zero}.\]

For an adjacent pair 0,0, the inner zero either produces zero or is already undefined. The outer zero therefore encounters 0^0 or an undefined exponent. Conversely, in a word with no adjacent zeros, every zero is followed by a positive-base suffix, which has a positive value whenever it is defined. Induction from the final token proves all powers are defined. A one cannot hide an undefined descendant.

julia> tower_isdefined([2,1,0,0])
false

julia> tower_isdefined([2,0,1,0,2])
true

julia> tower_language_counts(3)
(defined = 891, undefined = 9, total = 900)

This applies to tokens, not necessarily characters. Grouping 100 into one positive token is valid; the three-token tower 1^(0^0) is undefined. Addition changes the grammar, so the forbidden-pair criterion alone does not decide a mixed expression's validity.

Count enormous search spaces without searching them

For radix $q$, let $a_L$ count words ending in a nonzero digit and $z_L$ count words ending in zero. Extending a valid word gives

\[\begin{pmatrix}a_{L+1}\\z_{L+1}\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}q-1&q-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}a_L\\z_L\end{pmatrix}.\]

The initial vector is $(q-1,0)$ when leading zeros are forbidden, and $(q-1,1)$ for all fixed-width words. Binary matrix exponentiation computes the exact counts with $O(\log L)$ matrix products. It does not enumerate the $q^L$ strings.

Only about 0.0102% of 1000-digit decimal numerals have a defined tower under this convention. This is a count of all numerals, not a probability model for the structured primitive powers used by our search.

counts = tower_language_counts(1000)
@assert counts.defined + counts.undefined == 9big(10)^999
Float64(counts.defined // counts.total) # approximately 0.0001018573

Defining 0^0=1 makes every nonnegative-token tower defined. That seemingly local choice therefore changes an entire formal language.

Link the language to dynamics

The language count predicts how many states of modular_dynamics are undefined on their next step. It does not predict the basin of states that become undefined after several iterations.

At decimal width three, 981 of the 1000 padded words are initially defined. Nevertheless, 960 states eventually reach undefined; the other 40 flow into the cycle $456\leftrightarrow624$. A largely defined one-step map can have a very small surviving basin.

graph = modular_dynamics(3)
language = tower_language_counts(3; leading_zero=true)
@assert graph.undefined == language.undefined == 19
@assert graph.undefined_basin == 960

The same criterion is also a cheap search sieve. The single-digit sweep rejects undefined words before allocating arbitrary-precision suffix tokens, while preserving its original candidate counts and completeness semantics.

Reproduce exact word counts and a profile of primitive-power candidates with:

julia --project=. research/language_experiments.jl
